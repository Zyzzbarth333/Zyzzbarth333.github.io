Theseus V has no 2D LiDAR. Its ground filter keeps MID-360 returns 0.08 to 1.0 m up and Nav2 plans on them, on KISS-SLAM's 15 cm occupancy grid; an earlier build sliced the cloud into a 2D scan with pointcloud_to_laserscan. ■ occupied · ■ footprint · ■ cost · ■ latest scan

How Theseus V drives with Nav2. The planner searches the costmap outward from the rover (■ the cells it tried, ■ its front); a smoother rounds off its grid path (dotted) into the one it drives (red); Regulated Pure Pursuit steers for a point 0.8 m ahead on that path (● in the circle), slowing near obstacles. Cost (■) falls away over the 1.0 m inflation radius. When it stops making progress, the behaviour tree backs it up 0.3 m and plans again. ◆ places worth exploring.

    04 · Systems, running

    Two systems you can poke

    Airbrake controller, on my airbrake design

    scrub along the graph · drag the yellow target

    After a 2.5 s lockout past burnout, the controller predicts apogee every step and opens the brakes to land on the target; on the right is my FHC-1 airbrake module from its CAD, in the 30kAB layout C airframe from our OpenRocket model, its four flaps turning on their real hinges. Illustrative drag model, not the Hera study: there, three of my four controllers hit a 2,900 m target within about 4 m RMS on 177 reachable simulated flights.

    OSIRIS V0 on the logic analyser

    hover a part

    The buses are the real V0 netlist: the IMU on SPI1, the barometer and power monitor on I²C1, the microSD on SPI2, a UART out to a JST-PH port, and power from the XT30 through the INA228 shunt and arming key to the buck. Wire colours follow the IEC 60757 codes in the avionics wiring standard I drafted. Traffic timing is illustrative.

    Parts light up green while their bus is talking. Hover a numbered part to isolate its bus.

    06 · About

    The engineer

    I'm studying mechatronic engineering at UQ (BE(Hons)/ME, finishing in 2027), and I like building things that have to work outside the lab.

    At UQ Space I wrote the autonomy stack and ground control station for Theseus, our rover, and the team placed 1st of 17 at the 2026 Australian Rover Challenge. On the rocket side I wrote the flight firmware for HAVOC-2, our flight computer, and built COSMOS, the ground station we run at launches.

    I led the avionics team of about 25 until November 2026. As lead I set up its 4 subteams and wrote the avionics sections of the critical design review for the 2027 rover competition, along with a requirements verification matrix. I handed the lead over to make room for my research assistant role and a Master's placement in Semester 1, 2027. I've stayed on the team as an engineer, and I advise the new Lead and Deputy Lead. I'm still working on the rocket and rover ground control stations, PCB design and the Theseus VI autonomy stack.

    I also work part-time as a research assistant in UQ's School of EECS, on PX4 flight-control software and modelling. The part of engineering I enjoy most is modelling: taking a real system and building a model that predicts how it will behave.
