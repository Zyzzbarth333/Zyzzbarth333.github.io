Airbrake control simulation

UQ Space · simulation study · 2026

A Python simulation for airbrakes that trim a rocket's apogee to a target, built for UQ Space. On the 177 of 200 simulated flights of the team's Hera rocket where a 2,900 m target was reachable, three of the four controllers missed it by about 4 m RMS with perfect state knowledge. Everything is simulation: no airbrake has been built.

My role

The problem

Airbrakes let a rocket shed speed during its coast so it reaches a target apogee instead of overshooting it. Before building any hardware, the team needed to know which controller to fly and what it would take to fly it well.

What I did

  • I fixed the model first: the team's RocketPy model of Hera matched OpenRocket only because several of its errors cancelled. After my fixes it agrees with OpenRocket to within 0.3% on three reference cases. That is model against model, not against a flight.
  • I wrote four controllers, target solve, PI, bang-bang and model predictive control, and flew each on the same 200 randomised 6-DOF flights at 10 Hz, with actuator lag and the competition's deployment rules.
  • 213 test functions in 12 files cover the model and the controllers.

Result

Over the 177 flights where the target was reachable, with the controllers given perfect state knowledge, the RMS miss was 3.7 m for PI, 3.9 m for target solve, 4.0 m for MPC and 21.4 m for bang-bang. The top three are within 0.25 m of each other, so tuning mattered more than the choice of controller.

What went wrong

  • No brake data yet: the brake drag is an assumed flat-plate model, because there is no wind-tunnel or CFD data, so the brakes' real authority is uncertain.
  • The model flies low: real flights of the team's rockets went 2.6 to 5.8% higher than their simulations, so the model needs correcting against flight data before it can predict one.
